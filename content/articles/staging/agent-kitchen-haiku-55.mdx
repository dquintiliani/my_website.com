Anthropic just released Haiku 5.5, and on average it costs about 75% less to run than Haiku 4.5. That's the headline everyone is sharing. To me, the bigger story is how the whole 5.5 lineup has been tailored to fit inside an agentic system.

![The 5.5 Agent Kitchen: pixel-art illustration of Opus 5.5 as head chef, Sonnet 5.5 as line cook, and Haiku 5.5 as prep cook](/images/agent-kitchen-55.webp)

Here is how I like to picture it, as a kitchen:

- **Opus 5.5 is the head chef.** It sets the strategy, plans the menu, and owns the quality of everything that leaves the pass. Michelin-star kitchens don't have their head chef dicing onions.
- **Sonnet 5.5 is the line cook.** It takes the plan and does the bulk of the cooking: building, assembling, and delivering the work. Line cooks keep meals coming, and work with what the prep cooks provide.
- **Haiku 5.5 is the prep cook.** You can run several at once, chopping, portioning, and organizing the narrow sub-tasks at a fraction of the cost.

:::takeaway
The real story isn't that Haiku got cheaper. It's that every station in the kitchen now has a model built specifically for it. Your team just needs to figure out what to serve customers.
:::
